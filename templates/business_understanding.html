Business Understanding

Phase 1: Defining the problem, objectives, and project feasibility.

1. Problem Context

Urban noise pollution is a growing environmental and public health crisis in major Colombian cities. Prolonged exposure to high decibel levels generated by vehicular traffic, industrial zones, and commercial activities leads to stress, hearing impairment, and cardiovascular diseases.

Current monitoring systems are often static and lack predictive capabilities, making it difficult for city planners to implement proactive noise mitigation strategies based on urban mobility patterns.

2. Business Objectives

  • Primary Goal: Develop a robust Machine Learning system capable of predicting acoustic pollution levels based on mobility indices, vehicular flow, and temporal variables.
  • Secondary Goal: Classify noise levels according to the Colombian regulatory framework (Resolution 627/2006 MADS) to identify critical zones automatically.
  • Operational Goal: Deploy the predictive model via a web interface to allow urban planners to simulate congestion scenarios and their acoustic impact.

3. Questions the Model Must Answer

  • What is the expected noise level (in dB) in a specific city zone given current vehicular flow and average speed?
  • Is a particular residential or commercial zone likely to exceed the normative daytime/nighttime decibel limits?
  • How does the "Mobility Index" correlate with critical noise pollution events during peak traffic hours?

4. Expected Impact

By accurately modeling the relationship between urban mobility and noise generation, this project provides a data-driven tool for environmental agencies. The expected impact includes optimized traffic routing to reduce acoustic saturation, justified public policies for noise control, and an improved quality of life for citizens residing in historically critical zones.

5. Feasibility, Risks, and Limitations

Feasibility

Highly feasible. We have secured >1000 structured records from open Colombian data portals, encompassing decibel readings, sensor statuses, and mobility metrics.

Risks

Sensor calibration errors in raw data and potential memory limits on Render's free tier during model inference. Concept drift if urban traffic patterns change drastically.

Limitations

Limited to the cities present in the dataset. The model cannot account for unpredictable acoustic anomalies like protests or isolated construction sites.
