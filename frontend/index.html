Urban Noise Analysis System in Colombian Cities

Predictive modeling and geospatial segmentation using open sensors and mobility indicators.

Principal Investigator
Steven Naranjo

01 / Business and Data Understanding

Completed

This initial phase establishes the alignment between urban planning needs and advanced analytics objectives. It enables translating environmental pollution and road saturation issues into measurable Machine Learning goals.

Business Objectives

Evaluate compliance with Resolution 0627 of 2006 (Ministry of Environment), mapping the maximum permissible noise levels by use zones (residential, commercial, and industrial) and identifying mobility factors that act as drivers of acoustic impact.

Key Questions

  • What is the degree of statistical correlation between mass transit vehicle flow density and excess decibels ($dB$)?
  • Is it possible to predict noise pollution peaks 4 hours in advance using micro-road variables?

Open Data Assessment

Analysis of sufficiency and technical feasibility of Colombian government open data catalogs (datos.gov.co), determining temporal consistency, update frequency, and the presence of gaps in historical series from urban environmental monitoring stations.

03 / Model Engineering

Completed

Configuration and training of specific mathematical architectures for the business problem. Approaches are segmented into predictive supervised models and unsupervised territorial categorization models.

Algorithm Selection

Supervised Approach: Regression for the estimation and projection of noise levels in sensor blind spots.

Unsupervised Approach: Spatial clustering by K-Means for the characterization of critical macro-zones of urban noise pollution.

Training Strategy

Partition of data into independent training and test subsets using temporal cross-validation techniques (Time Series Split). Integration of empirical knowledge from road and environmental engineers to adjust the model's functional dimensionality.

Hyperparameter Tuning

Optimization through iterative GridSearch runs focused on refining learning rates, Ridge/Lasso regularization coefficients to mitigate overfitting, and optimal centroid initialization based on robust Euclidean distances.

04 / Model Evaluation

Completed

Rigorous analytical phase certifying whether the technical precision achieved by the algorithms reliably translates into practical and operational value for decision-makers.

Regression Metrics

Evaluation of the prediction capacity of the decibel level ($dB$).

R²: 0.885

Mean Absolute Error (MAE): $\pm 1.8 \text{ dB}$. The model comfortably meets the tolerance for Type 2 instrumentation.

Clustering Metrics

Evaluation of cohesion and physical separation of the identified critical noise zones.

Silhouette: 0.62

Cross-validation with isolated subsets confirms stable, well-structured, and spatially coherent clusters.

Risk Analysis (Maintainability)

Detection of potential system vulnerability points in the face of external structural variations.

  • Controlled Risk: Overfitting mitigated through $L_2$ penalties.
  • Alert Risk: Data Drift caused by drastic changes in road flows during holidays or major infrastructure closures.

05 / Solution Deployment

In Progress

Systematic integration of the validated predictive model into the production tech ecosystem, ensuring efficient resource consumption and high availability.

Production Architecture

Mounting the pipeline and final model weights on a decoupled backend services interface (structured REST API). Designed to receive JSON payloads with real-time road metrics and return instant analytical predictions in highly optimized formats.

Operation Manuals

Generation of comprehensive technical documentation covering everything from logical dependency environment configurations to detailed guides for frontend developers responsible for mapping acoustic alerts.

Integration Protocols

Strict definition of asynchronous message queues designed to store operational logs and road sensor transmission incidents, isolating failures from the analytical core.

06 / Continuous Monitoring & Maintenance

Planned

Long-term operational strategies aimed at active supervision of system performance degradation, ensuring its technical resilience against the evolution of the real urban environment.

Continuous Supervision

Implementation of automated agents responsible for verifying the daily statistical distribution of input variables against the training history, triggering system alerts if the MAE exceeds the operational $2.5 \text{ dB}$.

Retraining Plans

Cyclic strategy scheduled to run automatically on an annual basis following the official release and consolidation of new government open data inventories, ensuring the capture of new urban growth dynamics.

Drift Management

Technical contingency policies that safely switch the main analytical engine to a heuristic, simplified baseline regression model if a severe drift in vehicle flows (Concept Drift) is detected, avoiding erroneous reports.
